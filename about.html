What HALO is

HALO (Safety Harness for Dynamic Localization) is a dementia-safety research project at Missouri University of Science and Technology. It combines a home base station (Halo Anchor), a wearable tracker (Halo Tag), and a conversational scheduling system (Synthetic Memory with the VIVA voice assistant). The idea that ties them together is the Mobile Zone, or MoZo: a safe zone that widens along the route to a confirmed appointment, only for that appointment's time window.

The design principle behind the assistant is ask, don't assume. Halo collects what, when, where, who, and how long through conversation, reads the event back, and changes nothing until the person says yes. A wrong default is not an inconvenience here; it reshapes a safety boundary.

The four detectors

All four watch the same GPS fixes on the same days, so differences between them come from the detectors, not from luck.

  • Static geofence alerts whenever the tag leaves the home circle.
  • Learned routine also allows trips it has seen to the same place on the same weekday at least three times in four weeks of location history, a stand-in for commercial routine-learning trackers.
  • MoZo allows trips that match a confirmed calendar event: a corridor along the route, a zone around the destination, and a time window padded by travel time and a buffer.
  • MoZo + routine allows a trip if either of the two would.

Key assumptions

Each value below is the default in the code. Settings marked adjustable can be changed on the Experiment page; the rest are fixed.

Everyday outings

  • Five weekly habits, each happening 85% of the time: church on Sunday (9:00 AM, 90 min), the pharmacy on Monday (3:00 PM, 20 min), the grocery on Tuesday (10:00 AM, 45 min) and Saturday (10:30 AM, 45 min), and the community center on Thursday (1:00 PM, 2 h). Start times vary by about 10 minutes.
  • On about 30% of days there is also a one-off trip (clinic, a friend's house, a diner, or the bank), starting between 9:00 AM and 5:00 PM and lasting 45 minutes to 2 hours. It is dropped if it would start within four hours of that day's habit.
  • She leaves 3 to 12 minutes earlier than needed, parks 20 to 150 m from the address, and the visit ends anywhere from 5 minutes early to 25 minutes late. Driving covers 450 m a minute; walking, 55 m.

Wandering

  • Episodes start from home, last 45 to 110 minutes, and never overlap an outing. About 25% start at night, 35% in the late afternoon (3:00 to 7:00 PM, "sundowning"), and 40% during the day.
  • Each follows one of the four patterns described in dementia research, chosen with equal odds: direct, random, lapping, or pacing.

Conversation to calendar

  • Habits are mentioned less often than one-off trips, because people rarely announce routine errands. This is why MoZo alone misses habitual trips and the hybrid exists.
  • With "ask, don't assume", missing fields are filled by asking. With "fill in defaults", the assistant guesses one missing field: a 9:00 AM start, a one-hour length, or no location (which means no zone is built at all). A guess that happens to be right, like 9:00 AM for church, does no harm.
  • An extraction error shifts the event by one to three hours, earlier or later.
  • Halo's read-back happens only with "ask, don't assume". When it catches a wrong event, the event is corrected before it is saved.
  • Spurious events go to a random place, between 10:00 AM and 10:00 PM, for one hour.

Zones and alerts

  • A MoZo window opens before the event by the travel time plus the buffer, and closes the same amount after it ends. The destination zone reaches 60 m beyond the corridor half-width (210 m by default).
  • The learned routine uses the 28 days before the run. A place visited on the same weekday three or more times becomes an allowance from the earliest departure to the latest return seen, padded by the same buffer.
  • An alert fires after two consecutive out-of-zone GPS fixes (one per minute) and clears after ten in-zone fixes. A missing fix leaves the alert as it was.

How to read the metrics

False alarms per week counts alerts that start when no wandering is happening (an alert that starts during an episode, or within five minutes after it ends, counts as real).

Caught within 5 min is the share of wandering episodes that triggered an alert within five minutes of leaving the zone: the home circle, or 150 m from the venue for a walk away from a visit. Episodes that never leave the zone are not counted. Caught at all is the share that triggered an alert at any point before the episode ended (or within five minutes after). Avg. min to alert is the average delay from leaving the zone to the alert, over the episodes that were caught.

Zone open while home counts hours per week when an allowance window is open while Margaret is not on an outing. During those hours, wandering along that route could go unnoticed. It is the safety cost of a wider zone, and it is where hallucinated or injected events show up. The static geofence never opens, so it is always zero.

The range shown next to false alarms per week is a 95% bootstrap interval over days (400 resamples).

In this model every detector eventually notices someone leaving the area, so the meaningful differences are in false alarms, speed, and how long the zone is open. Real-world performance would depend on hardware, GPS quality indoors and outdoors, and real people's routines, none of which this site measures.

Sharing a run

The pages share no memory, so settings travel in the link. Because the engine is seeded, the same link always regenerates exactly the same days and the same numbers, on any computer. For example, this opens a day under "fill in defaults" with a 200 m corridor:

simulated-day.html?assistant=assume&W=200

On the Experiment page, change the settings and run, then use Watch a day from this run: that link carries every setting you changed, and it is the one to copy and share. The simulated day links back to the Experiment page with the same settings. A link with no settings uses the defaults above, which are the paper's configuration.

Only the adjustable settings can travel in a link: seed, days, assistant, gate, W, buf, R, sigma, detour, errand, captureHabit, wanderPerWeek, and spuriousPerWeek. assistant takes ask or assume, gate takes 1 or 0, and settings left at their default are left out of the link. Each is checked against the same range and step as its Experiment control. A setting the page does not recognize is ignored, and a value that is malformed, out of range, off its step, or repeated falls back to the default. Either way, the page says so above the results.
